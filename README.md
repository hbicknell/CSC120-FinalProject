## Additional Reflection Questions
 - What was your **overall approach** to tackling this project?
    My overall approach to tackling this project was to first imagine what I wanted it to look like. Then I identified my classes and shelled out each of those. Then I just started where the player would start, what did I want to happen when they started? Where did I want them to be able to go? What did I want them to be able to do? And I just built out the code this way, adding things to classes when I needed. Changing attributes or adding getters to be able to do what I wanted it to. 

 - What **new thing(s)** did you learn / figure out in completing this project?
    I figured out how to do a gameloop or well a do loop but I thought that was interesting! I figured out how to more efficiently interact with person typing/next line. 

 - Is there anything that you wish you had **implemented differently**?
 
    I wish I had implemented the dialogue a little more efficently. It just feels like these huge chunks of code, that's why I moved it to main. I wish I had made gameloop and main a little more defined/seperate. Main came about because I didn't want the huge chunks of words in the gameloop but it sort of devolved a little bit and holds some different things. 
    Also the move counter. I think ultimately I did not want this to be an action move based game but I received feedback that there should be a way for the player to 'lose' the game by moves. While I agree for the current implementation it adds another dimension play wise but I think if I went back I would get rid of it, I want this to be more of a game with puzzles that if the player gets stuck on it then they 'lose' but ultimately have to figure it out. I think a version of 'losing' would be death in combat but I think they would respawn.

 - If you had **unlimited time**, what additional features would you implement?
    I would flesh out all the other classes and add them in. I want the player to engage in combat with opponents. I wanted them to be able to go into shops in the town. I wanted to create a darkness barrier in the cave so they'd have to go find a key to a box an npc was tampering with to get a lantern. I would make explore more exciting. I would add more rooms to the cave. I would also expand the lore, I think I would make it multiple jobs, maybe even a plot around memories and escaping this shady adventures guild/company. I would have the player actually have to return to Ariela. I would also add more items and more puzzles. I wanted to also add in Agility or Strength and Intelligence or Wisdom instead of Strength or Intelligence, I would then have different checks that would reward you for/allow you to use the one you picked. I would want the players abilities to be changed by items, like a sword buffing up how much damage they can do or a potion that makes their wisdom go up. I wanted to add secret passages that only unlocked based on perception which would go off of wisodm. I would overall ust populate the world with mroe itnersting things. 

 - What was the most helpful **piece of feedback** you received while working on your project? Who gave it to you?
    Feedback was just general user experience. I got to see how people interacted with it and it gave me pointers on where I need to make instructions more clear.

 - If you could go back in time and give your past self some **advice** about this project, what hints would you give?
    The advice would be start smaller then you want to, it's often easier to build up then build down. I started to think my idea was too small so I kept trying to build it up but I ran out of time and while the game is pretty simple what matters is that it is playable and winnable. Also I was pretty intimidated by the player typing in and the overall options of player interaction so I think I would say it's easier then I think, and to consider that you could do a 'pick a number' command option if I wanted something that might help me implement more features I wanted.
